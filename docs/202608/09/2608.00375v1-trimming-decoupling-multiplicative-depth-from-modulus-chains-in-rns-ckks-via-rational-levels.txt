## Abstract 

The Residue Number System variant of CKKS (RNS-CKKS) has become the standard approach for practical approximate homo-morphic encryption due to its efficient modular arithmetic and NTT-based polynomial operations. However, existing RNS-CKKS schemes inherently couple the supported multiplicative depth with the predefined modulus chain, where each level transition requires consuming an entire modulus factor. Consequently, the available computation depth is determined by the granularity of the modulus chain, resulting in coarse-grained depth management. Recent work on Grafting [4] decouples scale factors from ci-phertext moduli, enabling more flexible precision management in RNS-CKKS. However, the multiplicative depth remains fundamen-tally constrained by the modulus chain structure. In this paper, we propose Trimming , a novel fine-grained level management mechanism that decouples multiplicative depth from modulus chains in RNS-CKKS via rational levels. The key idea is to introduce an auxiliary trimming modulus chain composed of smaller NTT-friendly modulus factors, which enables partial modulus transitions instead of directly discarding an entire modulus factor. By replacing conventional discrete level reductions with fine-grained modulus factor refinement, Trimming provides a rational-level abstraction beyond the traditional integer-based modulus chain representation. Our approach preserves the compatibility with existing RNS-CKKS arithmetic while enabling more flexible depth management and adaptive modulus transitions. Similar to Grafting, which ad-dresses the precision bottleneck, Trimming targets the depth bot-tleneck in RNS-CKKS and contributes toward a fully decoupled RNS-CKKS architecture. The proposed framework will be further validated through con-crete implementation and experimental evaluation to investigate its practical performance and computational overhead in real-world homomorphic encryption applications.

## 1 Introduction 1.1 Background 

The CKKS homomorphic encryption scheme has become the de facto standard for approximate homomorphic computation due to its high efficiency and support for real-number arithmetic. Mod-ern implementations adopt the Residue Number System (RNS) to represent the ciphertext modulus as a product of multiple pair-wise coprime primes, enabling efficient polynomial arithmetic, key switching, and rescaling. A fundamental operation in RNS-CKKS is ModDown , which re-duces the ciphertext modulus after homomorphic multiplication. In conventional RNS-CKKS, ModDown is implemented by perma-nently removing the highest RNS modulus from the modulus chain. Consequently, every ModDown operation consumes one modulus level, making the available multiplicative depth strictly determined by the initial modulus chain. Although this design greatly simplifies modulus management, it also introduces an inherent coupling between the effective ci-phertext modulus and the modulus chain. The ciphertext modulus can only evolve through a sequence of discrete predefined modu-lus levels, preventing flexible modulus scheduling and restricting applications such as adaptive bootstrapping and arbitrary modulus reduction. 

## 1.2 Related Work 

Several approaches have been proposed to improve modulus man-agement in CKKS homomorphic encryption. The original CKKS scheme performs modulus reduction by removing one RNS modulus during each rescaling operation, and this strategy has been adopted by most modern implementations, including HEAAN, OpenFHE, SEAL, and Lattigo. Recent studies have attempted to improve the flexibility of mod-ulus management. For example, Grafting introduces rational scale factors to decouple ciphertext scales from the predefined modulus chain, allowing more flexible scale scheduling without modify-ing the RNS basis. However, existing approaches still rely on the conventional ModDown mechanism, in which ciphertext modulus reduction is realized by discarding an RNS modulus. Consequently, the ciphertext modulus remains constrained by the discrete modu-lus levels determined during parameter generation. To the best of our knowledge, no existing work directly addresses the problem of performing ModDown through modulus replace-ment while preserving the original RNS structure. 

First Generation: HElib Modulus Switching (2011–2015). The first practical implementations of lattice-based homomorphic encryp-tion adopted modulus switching to control ciphertext noise and improve computational efficiency. HElib, introduced by Halevi and Shoup [8], employs the DoubleCRT representation to support effi-cient polynomial arithmetic and modulus switching. However, its modulus reduction relies on scale-and-round techniques, which require expensive large-integer operations and do not fully exploit the advantages of the Residue Number System (RNS). 

Full-RNS BFV (2016). Bajard et al. [2] introduced the first Full-RNS variant of the FV/BFV homomorphic encryption scheme. By representing ciphertexts entirely in the RNS domain, their ap-proach eliminates expensive CRT reconstruction and enables effi-cient ModUp/ModDown operations through fast basis conversion techniques. This work established the foundation for modern RNS-based homomorphic encryption libraries.

Original CKKS (2017). Cheon et al. [7] proposed CKKS, an ap-proximate homomorphic encryption scheme supporting efficient computation over real and complex numbers. The original CKKS construction performs rescaling through large integer division, where the ciphertext modulus and scale factor are reduced simulta-neously. The proposed approximate rescaling and modulus reduction techniques rely on fast basis con-version and approximate floor operations, enabling practical CKKS implementations with NTT-friendly RNS moduli. Lattigo. Lattigo [9] is an efficient Go-based homomorphic en-cryption library supporting BFV and CKKS schemes. It adopts ad-vanced RNS arithmetic and Shenoy–Kumaresan style basis con-version methods for efficient ModUp and ModDown operations. Similar to other Full-RNS implementations, Lattigo follows the tra-ditional modulus chain paradigm in which ciphertext levels are restricted to predefined modulus values. 

Summary of Full-RNS ModDown. Modern Full-RNS libraries, in-cluding HElib, HEAAN, OpenFHE, and Lattigo, mainly focus on improving the efficiency and accuracy of ModDown through tech-niques such as exact/approximate ModDown, basis conversion, and fast floor computation. However, existing approaches share the same limitation: after ModDown, the highest RNS modulus is per-manently removed from the modulus chain. Therefore, the available ciphertext levels and effective modulus values remain restricted to the discrete chain generated during parameter selection. In contrast, our proposed Trimming technique preserves the Full-RNS ModDown procedure while redesigning the modulus chain up-date strategy. Instead of discarding the last RNS modulus, Trimming replaces it with a smaller modulus, enabling continuous modulus adjustment without reducing the number of RNS components. Con-sequently, Trimming provides more flexible modulus scheduling. Existing works optimize how to perform ModDown, while Trim-ming changes what ModDown means by replacing modulus re-moval with modulus replacement. 

## 1.3 Our Contributions 

In this work, we propose Trimming , a new modulus management framework for RNS-CKKS homomorphic encryption. Unlike the conventional ModDown operation, which removes the highest RNS modulus, Trimming replaces the last modulus with a smaller modu-lus. As a result, the ciphertext modulus can be reduced continuously while maintaining the same number of RNS components. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. 

• We propose a novel Trimming operation that realizes Mod-Down by replacing the highest RNS modulus with a smaller modulus rather than discarding an RNS channel. This en-ables continuous modulus adjustment while preserving the overall RNS representation. 

• We demonstrate that Trimming decouples the effective ciphertext modulus from the predefined modulus chain. Consequently, ciphertexts are no longer restricted to the discrete modulus levels determined during parameter gen-eration, allowing much more flexible modulus scheduling. 

• We analyze the correctness and computational complexity of Trimming and discuss its compatibility with existing RNS-CKKS implementations. Although Trimming intro-duces additional computations compared with conventional ModDown, it significantly expands the design space of mod-ulus management for homomorphic encryption.

## 3.4 Fully Decoupled RNS-CKKS 

In conventional RNS-CKKS, the ciphertext modulus chain implicitly determines the available computation depth. Specifically, the mod-ulus chain is divided into discrete levels, and each level transition requires consuming one complete modulus factor. Therefore, the relationship among the modulus chain, logical level, and supported multiplicative depth can be represented as: Modulus Chain → Level → Multiplication Depth .

Such an inherent coupling makes multiplicative depth manage-ment coarse-grained. Even when a computation only requires a fraction of a modulus factor, conventional CKKS must consume an entire level from the predefined modulus chain. Consequently, the supported multiplicative depth is strictly constrained by the number and size of modulus factors selected during parameter generation. Trimming breaks this limitation by introducing fine-grained modulus factors and rational-level transitions. Instead of defining the computation depth solely according to the discrete modulus chain, Trimming introduces an intermediate level-factor abstrac-tion, where each conventional modulus factor can be further refined into multiple sub-level factors. Therefore, the relationship between modulus representation and computation capability is transformed into: Modulus Chain . Logical Level . Multiplication Depth In this new abstraction, the modulus chain only determines the underlying RNS representation, while the introduced level factors control the granularity of modulus consumption. As a result, the supported multiplication depth is no longer restricted by the dis-crete structure of the original modulus chain. This decoupling enables flexible depth management without modifying the fundamental RNS-CKKS arithmetic framework. In particular, Trimming allows a computation to consume only the required fraction of modulus resources, providing a fine-grained alternative to conventional level-based depth management. In addition to Trimming, Grafting represents another comple-mentary approach toward relaxing the inherent coupling in con-ventional RNS-CKKS. Grafting focuses on the relationship between the scale factor and the ciphertext modulus. In contrast, Trimming focuses on the relationship between the logical computation level and the ciphertext modulus chain. By introducing fine-grained modulus factors and rational-level transitions, Trimming enables flexible depth management without strictly consuming an entire modulus factor for each level transition. ## 4 Applications 

The proposed Trimming operation provides a flexible mechanism for modifying the effective ciphertext modulus without perma-nently shortening the modulus chain. Unlike the conventional RNS-CKKS scheme, where the modulus chain is fixed after parameter generation, Trimming allows the last RNS modulus to be replaced by a smaller modulus. Consequently, the ciphertext modulus can be adjusted continuously rather than discretely, enabling several applications that are not supported by the conventional ModDown operation. In this section, we demonstrate two representative applications of Trimming: flexible ModDown and bootstrapping.

## 4.2 Bootstrapping 

Bootstrapping refreshes a ciphertext by reducing accumulated noise and restoring the available multiplicative depth. The efficiency of bootstrapping is highly dependent on the ciphertext modulus used throughout the procedure. Conventional RNS-CKKS employs a fixed modulus chain, re-quiring the bootstrapping algorithm to operate on predetermined modulus levels. As a result, the ciphertext modulus before and after bootstrapping cannot be adjusted according to the computational requirements. Trimming removes this restriction by allowing the last RNS modulus to be replaced with an smaller modulus. Immediately before bootstrapping, the ciphertext modulus can be trimmed to the minimum value sufficient for evaluating the bootstrapping cir-cuit, thereby reducing the computational complexity of polynomial arithmetic and basis conversion. After bootstrapping, the trimmed modulus can be replaced with a larger modulus to restore the desired ciphertext precision without changing the remaining modulus chain. Therefore, Trimming provides a flexible interface between mod-ulus management and bootstrapping, allowing different stages of the bootstrapping procedure to operate under different effective modulus sizes while preserving compatibility with the original RNS basis. 

## 4.3 Discussion 

The proposed Trimming operation decouples the effective cipher-text modulus from the predefined modulus chain. Unlike conventional RNS-CKKS, where ciphertext moduli are restricted to the discrete values determined during parameter gen-eration, Trimming enables continuous modulus adjustment by re-placing the last RNS modulus with a smaller modulus. This capability naturally supports flexible ModDown, adaptive bootstrapping, and other modulus management techniques without requiring additional RNS levels or regenerating switching keys. Consequently, Trimming significantly improves the flexibility of modulus scheduling while maintaining compatibility with existing RNS-CKKS implementations.

## 6 Limitations 

Compared with the conventional ModDown operation, which re-moves the highest RNS modulus by simply dropping an RNS chan-nel, the proposed Trimming operation incurs additional compu-tational overhead due to the replacement of the highest modulus with a smaller one. Specifically, Trimming requires extra basis con-version and coefficient adjustment to preserve the correctness of the RNS representation. Therefore, its computational complexity is moderately higher than that of the conventional ModDown opera-tion. This additional overhead, however, enables flexible modulus scheduling, and adaptive bootstrapping, which are not supported by existing RNS-CKKS schemes. 

## 7 Conclusion 

In this paper, we proposed Trimming , a novel modulus management technique for RNS-CKKS homomorphic encryption. Unlike the conventional ModDown operation, which permanently removes the highest RNS modulus from the modulus chain, Trimming replaces the last modulus with a smaller modulus while preserving the overall RNS structure. This enables the effective ciphertext modulus to be adjusted continuously without reducing the number of RNS components. Based on this mechanism, Trimming decouples the effective ciphertext modulus from the predefined modulus chain, providing significantly greater flexibility for modulus scheduling. In particular, the proposed approach supports ModDown to target moduli instead of being restricted to the discrete modulus levels determined during parameter generation. Although Trimming incurs additional computational overhead compared with the conventional ModDown operation due to mod-ulus replacement and the associated RNS transformations, it sub-stantially expands the design space of modulus management in RNS-CKKS. The proposed framework is compatible with existing RNS-CKKS implementations and does not require modifications to the underlying homomorphic encryption scheme.
